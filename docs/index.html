Content Decay Intelligence

A Ranked Review Engine for Search Visibility

Noor Fatima  ·  FlyRank ML Internship  ·  August 2026  ·  GitHub Repo

Abstract

Content teams managing large page portfolios cannot manually review every page each week. This paper asks: which pages deserve attention first? Using an anonymized dataset of 30,000 pages from the FlyRank ML Internship, a Random Forest classifier was trained on non-leaking behavioral and structural signals to predict genuine click-drop decay. The model achieved Precision@50 of 0.74 under grouped client-level cross-validation, compared to a rule-based baseline of 0.72, producing a ranked review queue with reason codes and tiered action labels.

1. Introduction

Content teams manage hundreds of pages but only have time to review 20 to 50 per week. Without a system, deciding which pages to look at first relies on simple hand-written rules. This project builds a ranked review queue that scores pages by how urgently they need attention. A reviewer uses this list to decide which pages to refresh, expand, or flag for monitoring. If the list is wrong, the reviewer wastes time on pages that are fine, and pages that are actually declining don't get noticed.

2. Data

The FlyRank ML Internship starter dataset: 30,000 anonymized pages, 44 columns, covering impressions, clicks, sessions, CTR, average position, content age, word count, and engagement rate.

Excluded columns (label leakage prevention): trend_direction, trend_pct, clicks_last_30d, clicks_prev_30d, sessions_last_30d, sessions_prev_30d, client_id (grouping only).

Safe features used: ctr, avg_position, impressions_prev_30d, engagement_rate, content_age_days, word_count, days_since_last_update, plus engineered ratios ctr_to_position_ratio and engagement_density.

3. Methodology

Label: A page is a decay candidate if clicks_last_30d < clicks_prev_30d — a measurable drop with no label leakage.

Baseline: Hand-written rule flagging pages with content_age_days > 180 and declining impressions. Precision@50 = 0.72.

Model: Random Forest Classifier with GroupKFold cross-validation split on client_id. Hyperparameter tuning via RandomizedSearchCV optimizing Macro F1.

5. Limitations

All findings are observational and directional. This model does not establish causation between refresh actions and traffic recovery. Results may not generalize beyond this anonymized dataset. The model is a decision-support tool, not an automated publishing system.

6. Recommendations

Action Score > 0.80 (Top 50 Queue): Priority Refresh — update content, refresh meta tags, check broken links.

Action Score < 0.35 (High Traffic): Protect and Maintain — do not modify structure or URLs on high-performing evergreen pages.

Action Score > 0.80 + Low Impressions: Prune or Merge — consolidate via 301 redirects or remove to preserve crawl budget.
